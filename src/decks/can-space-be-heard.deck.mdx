# Can space be heard?

<p className="lede">Before we make the universe audible, we have to say what kind of “hearing” we mean.</p>

---

## One question, three claims

<div className="deck-grid three">
  <div className="panel gold">
    <p className="panel-title">Physical sound</p>
    <p>Pressure waves moving through matter. A medium is required.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="panel">
    <p className="panel-title">Measured signal</p>
    <p>Voltage, photon count, strain or intensity recorded by an instrument.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="panel warm">
    <p className="panel-title">Sonification</p>
    <p>A designed mapping that turns values or events into audible parameters.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p className="question">Which claim is being made when a headline says “NASA recorded the sound of space”?</p>

---

## Vacuum is not silence

<div className="deck-grid three">
  <div className="metric gold"><strong>0</strong><span>air molecules available to carry ordinary pressure waves in an ideal vacuum</span></div>
  <div className="metric"><strong>many</strong><span>measurable electromagnetic, particle and gravitational signals</span></div>
  <div className="metric warm"><strong>1+</strong><span>design decisions needed before those signals reach a speaker</span></div>
</div>

Space is not empty of **information**. It is usually empty of the medium our ears expect.

## Two legitimate routes to a speaker

<div className="deck-grid">
  <div className="panel gold">
    <p className="panel-title">Audification</p>
    <p>Replay an existing time series as audio, usually with a new speed or frequency range.</p>
    <p><strong>Preserves:</strong> temporal shape.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="panel">
    <p className="panel-title">Parameter mapping</p>
    <p>Assign data dimensions to pitch, duration, level, timbre or stereo position.</p>
    <p><strong>Reveals:</strong> selected relationships.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Neither route is neutral. Each supports a different scientific question.

## The accountable signal chain

<div className="process-flow">
  <div><strong>Observe</strong><span>Name the measured quantity, units and sampling.</span></div>
  <div><strong>Transform</strong><span>Expose cleaning, scaling, filtering and gaps.</span></div>
  <div><strong>Map</strong><span>State every data-to-sound relationship.</span></div>
  <div><strong>Test</strong><span>Ask whether a listener can answer the intended question.</span></div>
</div>

This four-part chain becomes the method for the entire semester.

## Rewrite the claim

<div className="deck-grid">
  <div className="choice warm">
    <p className="panel-title">Too strong</p>
    <p>“This is the sound of an exoplanet.”</p>
    <span>It hides source, processing and authorship.</span>
  </div>
  <div className="choice gold">
    <p className="panel-title">Checkable</p>
    <p>“Relative brightness samples were mapped linearly to pitch and played 320 ms apart.”</p>
    <span>A listener can reconstruct what happened.</span>
  </div>
</div>

Good disclosure does not make the experience less wondrous. It locates the wonder honestly.
