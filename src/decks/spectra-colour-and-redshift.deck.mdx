# Spectra, colour and redshift

<p className="lede">A spectrum is ordered by wavelength, not by time. Playback creates a timeline that never existed at the telescope.</p>

---

## A spectrum is already a relationship

<div className="deck-grid three">
  <div className="metric gold"><strong>x</strong><span>wavelength or frequency</span></div>
  <div className="metric"><strong>y</strong><span>flux, intensity or counts</span></div>
  <div className="metric warm"><strong>shape</strong><span>continuum, lines and noise</span></div>
</div>

The first design obligation is to preserve position along the spectral axis.

---

## From violet to red is not from past to future

<div className="spectrum-strip" role="img" aria-label="A visible-spectrum colour strip from violet through blue, green and yellow to red"></div>

Scanning left to right makes wavelength unfold as playback time. That is useful—but it is a convention, not a physical event sequence.

## Redshift is a comparison

> redshift = (observed wavelength − rest wavelength) / rest wavelength

<div className="deck-grid">
  <div className="panel gold"><p className="panel-title">Rest reference</p><p>A known spectral line at its laboratory wavelength.</p></div>
  <div className="panel warm"><p className="panel-title">Observed line</p><p>The corresponding feature shifted in the measured spectrum.</p></div>
</div>

Without the reference, a moved line is only a position—not a redshift claim.

## Three ways to render a shifted line

<table className="mapping-table">
  <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Reference cue</th><th>Measured cue</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>scan time</td><td>click at rest position</td><td>tone at observed position</td></tr>
    <tr><td>stereo</td><td>centre anchor</td><td>left / right offset</td></tr>
    <tr><td>pitch interval</td><td>fixed reference note</td><td>mapped comparison note</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

The listener needs both positions and the rule that relates their separation to wavelength.

---

## Reference cues need limits

<div className="deck-grid three">
  <div className="choice gold"><p className="panel-title">Known</p><p>laboratory line identity</p></div>
  <div className="choice"><p className="panel-title">Measured</p><p>peak position and width</p></div>
  <div className="choice warm"><p className="panel-title">Inferred</p><p>match and redshift estimate</p></div>
</div>

A musical consonance between notes is not evidence that the line identification is correct.

---

## Line width and uncertainty matter

<table className="mapping-table">
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Possible cue</th><th>Risk</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>peak position</td><td>event time</td><td>playback resolution</td></tr>
    <tr><td>line width</td><td>tone duration</td><td>confused with importance</td></tr>
    <tr><td>uncertainty</td><td>tremolo or boundary tones</td><td>decoration can dominate</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

The cue should help answer a spectral question, not merely make the sound richer.
