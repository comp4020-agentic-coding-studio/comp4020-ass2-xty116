# Spectra, colour and redshift

## The horizontal axis is not time

SLOP3255 · Week 8

---

## A spectrum is already ordered

Each sample pairs wavelength with measured flux.

Peaks may mark emission lines.

Dips may mark absorption.

Spacing along the axis carries physical information. Playing left to right
turns that axis into time.

---

## Preserve position before beauty

Mapping flux to pitch makes peaks sound high.

Mapping wavelength to pitch makes line position audible.

These are different questions.

A chord built from several lines may be memorable while obscuring their exact
spacing.

---

{/* _class: compact */}

## Redshift is a comparison

Spectral features move toward longer wavelengths.

To compare two spectra, keep:

- the same wavelength window
- the same scaling
- the same playback rate
- audible axis landmarks

Only then does a shifted cue support a shifted-line claim.

---

## Reference cues need limits

A spoken wavelength can orient precisely.

A drone can mark a reference line.

A click can mark regular axis intervals.

Too many cues mask the spectrum. Test the smallest set that supports the task.
