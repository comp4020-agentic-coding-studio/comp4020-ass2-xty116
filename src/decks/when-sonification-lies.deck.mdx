# When sonification lies

## A persuasive sound can make a weak claim feel certain

SLOP3255 · Week 11

---

## Every mapping has rhetoric

Rising pitch can suggest progress.

A major chord can suggest resolution.

Reverberation can imply physical space or scale.

Regular rhythm can hide irregular sampling.

These meanings are not measured values.

---

## Four quiet ways to mislead

**Selection:** play only the dramatic interval  
**Cleaning:** remove ambiguity without disclosure  
**Quantisation:** invent categories or steps  
**Decoration:** add musical structure that resembles evidence

The result can remain beautiful. The claim must remain bounded.

---

## Run the hostile reading

Before explaining your work, ask a listener:

1. What do you think was measured?
2. What changed in the source?
3. Which sound features seem physically meaningful?
4. How certain does the result feel?

Compare their inference with your actual pipeline.

---

{/* _class: compact disclosure */}

## The disclosure checklist

- source, object and retrieval date
- units and sampling
- cleaning and missing values
- scale, range and timing
- filtering and compression
- uncertainty treatment
- musical additions
- accessible alternatives

Disclosure does not excuse a poor mapping. It makes critique possible.
