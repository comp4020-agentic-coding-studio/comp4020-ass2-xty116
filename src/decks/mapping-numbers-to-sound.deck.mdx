# Mapping numbers to sound

## A mapping is a model

SLOP3255 · Week 3

---

{/* _class: compact */}

## Auditory dimensions are not interchangeable

Pitch supports ordered comparison.

Loudness is powerful but bounded by hearing safety.

Timbre can separate categories but is hard to quantify.

Stereo position suggests location but changes with listening equipment.

Time creates sequence even when the source had none.

---

## Linear or logarithmic?

A linear scale preserves equal numerical differences.

A logarithmic scale makes ratios easier to compare across a large range.

Musical pitch already feels logarithmic: doubling frequency is heard as an
octave.

Choose the scale for the task, not for familiarity.

---

## Continuous or quantised?

Continuous pitch preserves fine differences.

Quantised notes are easier to name and remember.

Quantisation can also invent steps that the data never contained.

The legend must name thresholds and rounding.

---

{/* _class: compact */}

## Test a question, not a vibe

Can a listener:

- locate the largest value?
- detect a repeated pattern?
- compare two rates of change?
- identify an outlier?

A mapping succeeds against a task. “It sounds cosmic” is not a task.
