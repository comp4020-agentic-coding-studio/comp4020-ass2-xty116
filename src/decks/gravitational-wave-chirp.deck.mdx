# The gravitational-wave chirp

## The sound is a transformation

SLOP3255 · Week 6

---

## Start with a claim

On 14 September 2015, LIGO's two detectors observed a signal consistent with
the merger of two black holes.

The event is **GW150914**.

The detector output is strain over time, not a microphone recording.

---

## The measured object

**Strain** is relative change in arm length.

The signal is:

- very small
- mixed with detector noise
- time-indexed
- independently observed at two sites

The useful question is not “what did space sound like?” but “what operations
make this strain pattern audible?”

---

## Why a chirp?

As the black holes spiral together:

1. their orbital frequency rises
2. the gravitational-wave frequency rises
3. the signal amplitude grows
4. merger ends the rising pattern

Frequency increase belongs to the event. Playback volume does not.

---

## The signal chain

**interferometer strain**

→ calibration  
→ band-pass and noise treatment  
→ event window  
→ amplitude scaling  
→ optional frequency shift  
→ speaker

Every arrow changes what can be heard.

---

## Three versions, three claims

**Raw window**  
Closest to the calibrated series; difficult to hear.

**Filtered and amplified**  
Pattern is clearer; filtering must be disclosed.

**Frequency shifted**  
Easier for some listeners; pitch no longer occupies the measured band.

Clarity and fidelity are related, not identical.

---

## Inspect more than audio

A responsible presentation pairs sound with:

- strain values and units
- a time-domain plot
- a spectrogram
- processing parameters
- detector and event provenance

No channel has to carry the whole argument.

---

## What uncertainty sounds like

Noise is not an embarrassing border around the signal.

It is part of the detection problem.

Compare the event window with nearby detector data. Ask which features persist,
which disappear after filtering and which cannot be assigned confidently.

---

## Disclosure before drama

Before publishing, name:

- source and retrieval date
- selected time interval
- filters and thresholds
- speed or frequency changes
- amplitude normalisation
- omitted or interpolated data
- added musical material

If a listener cannot reconstruct the operation, the legend is incomplete.
