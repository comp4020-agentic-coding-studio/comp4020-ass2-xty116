# Accessibility beyond the graph

## Sound adds a route. It does not replace every route.

SLOP3255 · Week 10

---

{/* _class: compact */}

## Sonification can expand access

Sound may reveal trends without requiring visual inspection.

It can support blind and low-vision learners.

It can also exclude Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, sensory-sensitive users,
or anyone unable to play audio.

Access is a system, not a format.

---

{/* _class: compact */}

## One task, coordinated channels

Audio: hear the central dip

Visual: inspect the plotted curve

Table: read every value and unit

Description: understand the pattern and limits

The scientific conclusion should survive when any one channel is unavailable.

---

{/* _class: dense */}

## Control belongs to the listener

Every instrument needs:

- keyboard-operable transport
- immediate stop
- user-controlled level and speed
- no autoplay
- reduced-motion support
- visible focus and status

Control is part of meaning, not interface polish.

---

## Equivalent does not mean identical

A transcript of pitches may not explain a trend.

Alt text may not carry every value.

Provide the representation that supports the same task, then test that task in
audio-only, visual-only and keyboard-only passes.
